Renewal Decision Brief: a working prototype for the intelligent repository

For every signed supplier contract it answers one question for procurement: what is the last safe date to say no before this renews itself, what happens if we do nothing, and how do we know?

1. Why this stage

The brief's three results (unreviewed autorenewals, missed service levels, invoices that do not match agreed prices) all happen after signature. Renewal is the one with a dated, one-way cost: miss the notice date and the company is committed for another term. It is also checkable: a date is right or wrong. Real contracts make the date hard to find, which is why AI helps and why it is risky: terms often run from an event no document dates, amendments change them, and notice must go by a set method.

What already exists, and what this adds

Checked on 22 Nov 2026 from public pages. Sirion describes configurable renewal alerts with routing and escalations, and its own guidance tells teams to confirm the notice method, keep proof of delivery and review unclear terms before trusting an automated deadline. Ironclad launched a Renewal Agent that writes "Renewal Briefs" on 15 May 2026; this prototype's name overlaps with it. Gatekeeper times alerts to notice periods and adds spend, scorecards, renegotiation and exit-risk agents. Zoho Contracts has reminders, a calendar, renewal approvals and letter templates. So reminders, extraction and renewal briefs are standard. What this adds: a date is confirmed only when code finds every passage behind it, otherwise one named person is asked; the notice itself is checked for method and deemed receipt; the Excel tracker procurement already keeps is checked row by row on day one; and reminders follow the safe send date, not the end date.

2. Stakeholder map

  • Procurement (primary user): no surprise renewals, leverage before the deadline. Owns the record and sends notices.
  • Budget owner (second user): keep the service running with little admin. Answers one question at a time and makes the decision.
  • Legal: correct readings, only real ambiguity escalated. Sees conflicts with both passages side by side.
  • Finance: spend that matches contracts. Its payments file exposes suppliers paid with no contract on file.
  • Supplier (not a user): benefits from silence. The tool never contacts suppliers.

Conflicts: procurement wants decisions early, owners decide late; sending notice to renegotiate can unsettle a supplier the owner depends on; owners know when service really began but may misremember; Legal would read everything, procurement needs answers this month.

3. The problem, and how I know it is real

Illustrative sizing, built only on my assumptions except the 2,500 suppliers given in the brief: about 120 renewals a year that nobody decides, about ₹18 crore committed by silence each year, about ₹81 lakh of it avoidable, and about 210 questions a year to budget owners (roughly 4 a week). The interactive version lets you change every assumption.

Public filings show terms that start from a date the supplier sets by notice, amendments that change it, and notice that must go by registered mail to a separate address. State audits show rates that did not match contracts because nobody, or the wrong system, checked. I have not interviewed a practitioner; the prototype says so and names the first discovery step.

4. The working prototype

  1. Procurement adds a signed contract and its amendments as one family.
  2. Claude reads it and quotes the exact passage behind each fact. Plain code checks each passage is really in the document, then works out the safe send date, the contractual deadline and a decide-by date.
  3. If a date depends on something outside the documents, no date is confirmed: one named owner gets one question, and odd answers go back to procurement to check.
  4. The owner records renew, renegotiate or exit. Procurement records what was actually sent; a wrong method, or a notice that counts as received outside the contract's window, stays open.
  5. The portfolio ranks every contract by urgency and money, and says what it does not cover.
  6. Reminders follow the safe send date, stop once a valid notice is recorded, and download as a calendar file.
  7. On day one, the Excel tracker procurement already keeps is checked against the contracts: on the sample, 4 of 8 rows would miss or mislead a renewal.

5. Success metrics

North star: of the annual value of renewals due in the period, the share where the named owner decided in time and any needed notice or extension was valid and received by the deadline. Paid spend with unknown renewal status is reported beside it. Rethink if: a lawyer finds any wrong confirmed date in a 50-contract check; most contracts need a person anyway; most at-risk value has no contract on file; or owners ignore questions even when escalated.
